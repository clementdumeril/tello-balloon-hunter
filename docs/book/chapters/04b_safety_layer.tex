\chapter{Nominal pipeline and safety layer}
\label{ch:safetylayer}

The code grew in two directions at once. One part is what the drone \emph{needs} to fly a mission in a fixed,
well-prepared room: a link to the drone, a pose from the markers, a filter, a model and a controller. The other part
was added flight after flight, each time something went wrong: a drift into the furniture, a lost video stream, a
second program sending commands. When reading the code it is easy to mix the two. This chapter separates them.

\begin{intuition}
Think of the \emph{nominal pipeline} as the answer to ``what is the shortest program that flies the demo on a good
day?''. Every line that is not in it is a \emph{guard}: it does nothing while things go well and takes over when they
do not. Every guard can be switched off by a parameter, and every one of them exists because of a real incident,
dated below.
\end{intuition}

\section{The nominal pipeline}

On a fixed setup (markers glued where \file{config/markers.yaml} says, camera calibrated, drone on its pad, nobody
else on the Wi-Fi, a cool battery) one command goes through five pieces:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.8cm}p{4.6cm}p{6.9cm}@{}}
\toprule
Piece & File & What it does on a good day \\
\midrule
camera $\to$ pose & \file{aruco\_localizer.py} & detect markers, joint PnP, one pose per image (Ch.~\ref{ch:aruco}) \\
pose $\to$ state & \file{core/ekf\_core.py},\newline \file{core/ekf\_node.py} & fuse delayed poses with the commands sent, 50~Hz state (Ch.~\ref{ch:ekf}) \\
model & \file{core/model.py} & $K$, $\tau$, $L_u$: how a command becomes a velocity (Ch.~\ref{ch:ident}) \\
state $\to$ command & \file{missions/tour.py} (\code{goto}),\newline \file{missions/strike.py} & error to a goal $\to$ body-frame velocity (Ch.~\ref{ch:missions}) \\
command $\to$\newline drone & \file{core/driver.py} & velocity $\to$ \code{rc} integers, 20~Hz, video and telemetry back (Ch.~\ref{ch:driver}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Plus the offline preparation: the marker map and the room scan (Ch.~\ref{ch:mapping}), and the camera
calibration. Nothing else is needed to fly. In particular \file{core/safety.py} could be replaced by the identity,
and \code{Mission.safety()} by \code{pass}: on a perfect day the flight would be the same.

\section{The safety layer, feature by feature}

The guards live at three levels, from the outermost (closest to the drone) inwards. A guard at a lower level
protects against \emph{every} program above it, including the teleop and a buggy mission; that is why the most
important ones are in the driver.

\begin{keypoint}
For a test on a clean, fixed setup one can relax the layer, never remove it: the guards cost nothing while they do
not trigger. The two that \emph{do} change a nominal flight are the position hold (it replaces a zero command by a
hold) and the wall guard (it slows the last 15~cm before a marker). A strike that must touch a balloon close to a
wall therefore runs with a smaller \code{min\_wall\_distance}, not without the guard.
\end{keypoint}

\section{Testing a guard without a drone}

The simulator (\file{sim/}) reproduces the incidents so that each guard can be tested on the fake drone. For the
camera failsafe, \file{sim/fake\_tello.py} stops delivering new images while the file named by the environment
variable \code{FAKE\_VIDEO\_OFF} exists:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
export FAKE_VIDEO_OFF=/tmp/video_off
touch $FAKE_VIDEO_OFF                       # before takeoff: the takeoff is refused
ros2 launch tello_driver real.launch.py mock:=true
rm $FAKE_VIDEO_OFF                          # video back: takeoff accepted
touch $FAKE_VIDEO_OFF                       # in flight: hover after 1 s, "FAILSAFE ... landing" after 5 s
\end{lstlisting}
The telemetry topic carries \code{video\_age} (seconds since the last image) to watch it live.
